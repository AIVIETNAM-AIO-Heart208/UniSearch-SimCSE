\subsection{Learning Sentence Embeddings for Similarity}
\label{sec:related-embeddings}

InferSent~\cite{conneau-etal-2017-supervised} used natural language inference
(NLI) as a source of supervision. Its encoder maps a premise and a hypothesis
separately to vectors, which a classifier uses to predict entailment,
contradiction or neutrality. Sentence-BERT (SBERT)~\cite{reimers-gurevych-2019-sentence}
applied this idea to BERT with shared encoder weights. At retrieval time,
each passage can be encoded once and compared with a new query by cosine
similarity. Supervised SimCSE~\cite{gao-etal-2021-simcse} keeps this property
and uses the NLI labels to train the similarity score directly. For a given
premise, the encoder learns to rank an entailed hypothesis above a
contradictory one.

Another line of work starts with a fixed encoder. Li et
al.~\cite{li-etal-2020-sentence} observed that BERT sentence vectors
concentrate along a few directions. Their BERT-flow method learns an
invertible transformation to map this distribution towards an isotropic
Gaussian. Su et al.~\cite{su2021whiteningsentencerepresentationsbetter}
used whitening, a linear transformation of centred vectors based on their
covariance. Both methods can be fitted on unlabelled sentences. In their
analysis of these baselines, Gao et al.~\cite{gao-etal-2021-simcse} found
that the vectors spread out more evenly but semantically related pairs
also moved farther apart. This helps explain why SimCSE explicitly rewards
similarity within each positive pair.

Without NLI labels, the choice of a positive pair determines what counts as
similar during training. DeCLUTR~\cite{giorgi-etal-2021-declutr} samples nearby
spans from one document, so a passage and a shorter span within it can form a
positive pair. Contrastive Tension~\cite{carlsson2021semantic} trains two
encoders with separate parameters to distinguish copies of the same sentence
from randomly paired sentences. Unsupervised SimCSE uses one encoder with independent
dropout masks on two passes over the same words. The other sentences in the
batch supply the negatives.

Under the same BERT-base evaluation in Table~5 of~\cite{gao-etal-2021-simcse},
Contrastive Tension and unsupervised SimCSE average 72.05 and 76.25,
respectively, in Spearman correlation multiplied by 100. These scores measure
agreement with human judgements of sentence similarity. Our retrieval task
instead asks which article contains the clause that answers a question.
