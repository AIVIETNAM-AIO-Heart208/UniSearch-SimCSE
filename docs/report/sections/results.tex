\subsection{English Sentence Similarity}
% Measured and paper values: experiments/reproduction/results/R1/metrics.json,
% experiments/reproduction/results/R2/metrics.json, experiments/reproduction/results/R3/metrics.json.
Table~\ref{tab:sts-reproduction} gives the mean Spearman correlation
across the seven STS tasks under the paper's \emph{all} protocol. The supervised
run matches the official repository's mean and differs from the paper by
0.01 points. The reproduced ordering holds on these English benchmarks.

The BERT-base baseline measures how well pretrained
BERT ranks sentence pairs when its first and last layers are averaged.
Unsupervised SimCSE improved the seven-task mean by 19.55 points over
this baseline using contrastive training on unlabeled sentences and a
different pooling procedure. Supervised SimCSE gained a further 5.32
points. This comparison changes both the training corpus (Wikipedia
versus SNLI and MNLI) and the construction of training pairs, so it
cannot isolate the effect of labels alone.

The gains also depend on the task. In Appendix~E, Gao et
al.~\cite{gao-etal-2021-simcse} evaluated seven classification tasks by
training a logistic regression classifier on frozen sentence embeddings.
They found no clear performance trend for unsupervised SimCSE across
these tasks despite its higher mean correlation on STS.

Vietnamese regulation retrieval poses a further test. Two articles might both describe academic
warnings, yet one might apply to first-year students and the other to
students in later years. A semantically similar passage can therefore
be ranked highly even when its eligibility rule does not apply. We can test this
by retrieving labelled passages for Vietnamese questions.

\subsection{Retrieval on D1-dev}
\label{sec:d1-dev-results}
% Query count and retrieval results: experiments/external_dataset/results/D1-dev-tuning/summary.json.
We compared BM25, SimCSE-VN and Hybrid on the 224 development questions described in
Section~\ref{sec:d1-setup}. Hit@$k$ is the proportion of questions whose
reference passage appears in the first $k$ results. MRR@10 is the mean
reciprocal rank of the reference passage, with a contribution of zero when
that passage is outside the first ten results.
% Manual BM25 verification: experiments/external_dataset/results/E0/d1-dev.md.
For query~9542, the independently recomputed BM25 scores reproduced the
saved first twenty passages, with the reference passage at rank two.

The sweep selected $\lambda=0.6$. The threshold $\theta=0.6201629042625427$

development scores describe the split used for selection.

% Dense case examples: experiments/external_dataset/results/D1-dev/runs/dense.jsonl.
In the SimCSE-VN run, Article~21 ranked first for query 1034, which asks
whether a decision to amend a diploma or certificate records the changes
and their reasons. Its highest-scoring chunk, Clause~1, explicitly lists both the
changes and their reasons among the required contents of the decision.
For query 1185, which asks whether a university may refuse to issue a copy
of a diploma, the system ranked a clause on dismissal from a combined
bachelor's--master's programme first. The labelled passage, Article~27 on
procedures for issuing copies from the original register, was outside the
top 20.

% Dense Hit@3: experiments/external_dataset/results/D1-dev/metrics.json.
SimCSE-VN returned the labelled passage among its first three suggestions
for 147 of the 224 D1-dev questions (Table~\ref{tab:d1-dev-dense}).
Hit@3 here measures passage retrieval on D1-dev. The English STS results use
Spearman's $\rho$ to measure rank agreement with human similarity
judgments across sentence pairs.

% Paired intervals and resampling settings: experiments/external_dataset/results/E1/d1-test.json.
Hybrid improved on BM25 by 2.11 percentage points in Hit@1 (95\% CI
$[0.39, 3.82]$) and by 0.018988 in MRR@10 (95\% CI $[0.008363, 0.029811]$).
We estimated both intervals from the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles
of 1,000 bootstrap differences in means, using paired question-level
resampling with replacement and seed 42.

% Coverage and group Hit@1: experiments/external_dataset/results/E1/d1-test.json.
At the fixed threshold selected on D1-dev, Hybrid marked 618 of 759
questions as confident (81.42\% coverage). Hit@1 was 76.05\% on those
questions and 56.74\% on the 141 questions with a warning. All D1-test
questions have reference passages, so the out-of-scope false-confidence
rate is undefined on this split. The threshold was calibrated on
ViRHE4QA passages and questions. Its coverage and false-confidence rate
on HCMUS questions remain to be measured.

% Coverage, risk and counts: experiments/external_dataset/results/E3/d1-test.json.
% Differences and warning counts are derived from operating_point and full_coverage.
At the fixed threshold in Figure~\ref{fig:d1-risk-coverage}, Hybrid marked
618 of 759 D1-test queries as confident (81.42\% coverage). The top-ranked
passage was incorrect for 148 of those queries (23.95\% risk), compared with
209 of all 759 queries at full coverage (27.54\%). With the ranking fixed,
the threshold trades 18.58 percentage points of coverage for a decrease
of 3.59 percentage points in the observed top-1 error rate among confident
results. The 141 warned queries accounted for 61 of the 209 top-1 errors.

\paragraph{Lexical overlap}
% Grouping, counts, metrics and intervals: experiments/application/results/E2/d1-test.json.
We measured the Jaccard overlap between the set of lowercased Pyvi word
tokens in each question and the set in its full labelled passage, excluding
punctuation-only tokens. The one-third and two-thirds quantiles of overlap
define the three D1-test groups in Table~\ref{tab:d1-overlap}.

% Group sizes and MRR@10 values: experiments/application/results/E2/d1-test.json.

% Paired intervals and distinct-token counts: experiments/application/results/E2/d1-test.json.
In the low-overlap group, Hybrid gained 4.74 percentage points in Hit@1
(95\% CI $[0.79, 8.70]$) and 0.042031 in MRR@10
(95\% CI $[0.018749, 0.065467]$) over BM25. Both intervals include zero
in each of the other groups. Across questions, the median number of
distinct tokens in the reference passage was 129 in the low-overlap
group and 55 in the high-overlap group. A longer labelled passage can
lower the Jaccard score by increasing the size of the union.

\paragraph{Error analysis}
% Counts, ranks and source excerpts: experiments/application/results/E5/d1-test.md.
Hybrid missed the labelled passage at rank one for 209 of 759 questions.
For 123 of those questions, the labelled passage was second or third.
We examined a purposive sample of 20 misses across rank ranges and baseline
outcomes. For query 8682 on the IELTS exemption threshold, Hybrid returned
placement-test scores. For query 5272,
Hybrid ranked the provision granting access to edit exam marks first.
BM25 and SimCSE-VN placed that provision third and fourth, respectively.
For query 4251, the reference passage directs readers from Article~10 to
Article~6 for the maximum duration. Hybrid returned the cited provision
in Article~6 first. The passage-ID metric counts this match as a miss.
